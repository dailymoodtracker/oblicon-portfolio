Introduction to Law

Law means any rule of action or system uniformity.

Characteristics of law

  1. It is rule of conduct - Law tell us what shall be done and shall not be done.
  2. It is obligatory - Law is considered a positive command imposing a duty to obey and involving sanction which forces obedience,
  3. It is promulgated by legitimate authority - Authority to make laws is conferred upon those duty chosen by the sovereign will of the people.
  4. It is common observance & benefit - Law must be observed by all for the benefit for all.

Classification of Law

As to its purpose:

  1. Substantive Law - The body of law creating, defining, and regulating right and duties.
  2. Asjective Law - Body of law prescribing the manner or procedure by which rights may be enforced or their violations redressed.

As to its subject matter:

  1. Public Law - Body of legal rules which regulates the rights and duties arising from the relationship of the state to the people.
  2. Private Law - body of rules which regulates the relations of individuals with one another for pureply private ends.

Sources of Law

  1. Constitution - The supreme law of the land. All other laws must conform to it.
  2. Legislation - Law eneacted by the legislative body (e.g., Congress, Republic act.
  3. Administrative or executive orders - Ruled issued by administrative agencies to implement and execute statutes.
  4. Judicial decisions or jurisprudence - Decisions of the supreme court interpreting laws, forming part of the legal systen.
  5. Customs - Habits and practices observed by a community for a long period, acknowledged as binding rules of conduct.
  6. Other sources - Supplementary and are resorted to by the courts in the absence of all other sources but are not binding on the courts.
