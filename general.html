General Provision of Obligation

An obligation is juridical necessity to give, to do or not to do.

Essential requisites of an obligation

  1. Passive Subject(Debtor) - the person who is bound to the fulfillment of the obligtion.
  2. Active Subject(Creditor) - The eprson who is entitled to demand the fulfillment of the obligation.
  3. Object or prestation(Subject matter of the obligation) - The conduct required to be observed by the debtor(to give, do, or not to do).
  4. Juridical or Legal tie(also called efficient cause) - That which binds or connects the parties to the obligation.
ART. 1157

Sources of Obligations

  1. Law - When they are imposed by law itself.
  2. Contracts - When they arise from the stipulation of the parties.
  3. Quasi-contracts - Lawful acts to prevent unjust enrichment.
  4. Delicts - When they arise from civil liability which is the consequence of a criminal offense.
  5. Quasi-delicts - Damages caused by negligence without a contract..

Kinds of Obligation

  1. Real Obligation - An obligation where the debtor is bound to deliver a specific or generic physical object to the creditor.
  2. Personal Obligation - An obligation where the subject matter is a human act, conduct, or service.
    • Positive personal obligation - Obligation to do or to render something.
    • Negative personal obligation - Obligation not to do.
ART. 1159

Obligations Arising from Contracts

"Obligations arising from contracts have the force of law between the contracting parties and should be complied with in good faith."

Force of Law

The contract is binding between the parties and must be fulfilled with the same obligation as a law passed by the legislature. Neither party can unilaterally withdraw or violate terms without legal liability.

Compliance in Good Faith

Performance must be done with fairness, honesty, and sincerity according to the true intent and spirit of the agreement, avoiding evasion or intentional breach.
