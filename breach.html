Remedies for Breach of Obligation

ART. 1207

Joint & Solidary Obligations

"The concurrence of two or more creditors or of two or more debtors in one and the same obligation does not imply that each one of the former has a right to demand, or that each one of the latter is bound to render, entire compliance with the prestation. There is a solidary liability only when the obligation expressly so states, or when the law or the nature of the obligation requires solidarity."

Joint Obligation

An obligation where each debtor is liable only for their proportionate share of the debt, or each creditor is entitled only to their proportionate share of the credit.

Solidary Obligation

An obligation where any one of the debtors can be compelled to pay the entire debt, or any one of the creditors can demand full payment from any debtor.

ART. 1311, Par.2

Stipulation Pour Autrui

"If a contract should contain some stipulation in favor of a third person, he may demand its fulfillment provided he communicated his acceptance to the obligor before its revocation."

Concept

An obligation that is capable of partial performance. The prestation can be executed in parts without destroying its essence or value.

ART. 1226

Obligation with a Penal Clause

"In obligations with a penal clause, the penalty shall substitute the indemnity for damages and the payment of interests in case of noncompliance, if there is no stipulation to the contrary."

Penal Clause

An accessory undertaking attached to an obligation to insure its performance by imposing a penalty or liability in case of breach or non-fulfillment.

General Rule on Penalty

The penalty automatically replaces the payment of damages and interest, unless expressly agreed otherwise or if the debtor refuses to pay the penalty.

Extra-Judicial Remedies & Judicial Remedies

Extra-Judicial Remedies

Measures resorted to by the aggrieved party on their own initiative without going to court (e.g., self-help, withholding performance, or rescission).

Judicial Remedies

Legal actions brought before the court to enforce fulfillment, demand rescission with damages, or seek specific recovery.
